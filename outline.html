Overview

AudienceDelivery leads, product owners, reporting authors, QA leads, engineers and approved Claude users.
Duration6 hours of instruction and practice. One full day or two half-days, with breaks added.
DeliveryBlended. Live virtual teaching, demos and sandbox exercises, with 30 minutes of on-demand preparation.
PrerequisitesIntermediate skills or equivalent evidence of verified JQL, sprint reporting, Plan capacity and Zephyr execution. Use the approved Claude client. Engineers completing the Git exercise also need basic branch, commit and review skills.
ScenarioFictional Crew Scheduling scenario. The release review is approaching. Plans shows a capacity risk, two reports disagree, most tests pass and a code change has no Jira link. Learners investigate whether the evidence supports release.

Learning objectives

  • Trace Initiative and Epic decisions through delivery, monitoring and measured business outcomes.
  • Compare Plan scenarios and explain capacity, dependency and release trade-offs.
  • Reconcile a Jira report with an eazyBI measure using definitions, import timing and item evidence.
  • Evaluate test coverage, failed or unexecuted tests and retest evidence for a release decision.
  • Connect Jira and an approved Git provider and verify development traceability.
  • Use Claude through Atlassian MCP, validate generated JQL and identify metric and permission risks.

Module detail

Connect outcomes to the forecast · Modules 1–2 · 90 minutes

1 · Trace portfolio work and business outcomes 35 min15 min practice

Objective. Explain the decision and evidence needed at an Initiative or Epic stage.

Topics. New Idea, Discovery, Technical Analysis, Prioritized, Developing, Monitored, Done and Canceled in the Crew example. Expected Business Impact, Lean Canvas link, PPM reference, start date and outcome evidence where the template uses them. Portfolio T-shirt sizing, team story estimates and Initiative permissions. Risk treatment as Resolved, Owned, Accepted or Mitigated, plus LeadershipRequest labeling for the approved leadership view.

Instructor demo. Follow a seeded Epic from its expected business benefit through delivery and Monitored (CRW-1 → CRW-5). Show an Impediment (CRW-46) and the leadership view using a practice label.

Practice: close the work or keep monitoring. Inspect an Epic with delivered child work but no measured outcome (CRW-5). Identify the evidence needed before closing it, plus a risk owner and an escalation if required. Explain how portfolio sizing and team estimates support their respective decisions.

Learner output. A justified portfolio stage and a short list of outcome evidence, risk treatment and ownership still needed.

2 · Compare Plans scenarios across teams 55 min30 min practice

Objective. Recommend a delivery scenario based on capacity, dependencies, releases and stated assumptions.

Topics. Plan sources, hierarchy, team setup, capacity, sprint assignments and scheduled dates. Alternative scenarios, dependency warnings, cross-team release coordination and uncertain or unestimated work. Draft changes and a reviewed selection of updates before saving to the training space.

Instructor demo. Duplicate the baseline practice scenario. Reduce Swap Squad's capacity for SWP-2026.q4.5 from 20 to 15, inspect the dependent Alerts Squad activity (CRW-73) and compare shifting scope with changing the handoff.

Practice: choose a realistic release forecast. Build two options for the reduced-capacity team using the supplied scenario inputs. Check both teams' sprint load, dependency order and target date for each option. Recommend an option with its assumptions. Review draft changes; on the training site no update is designated, so learners discard.

Learner output. Two scenario views and a recommendation tied to capacity, dependency order and the release target.

Reconcile the delivery evidence · Modules 3–4 · 90 minutes

3 · Reconcile eazyBI and Jira delivery metrics 50 min25 min practice

Objective. Find the reason for a report discrepancy before using the number in a delivery decision.

Topics. Sprint dimensions, committed and completed measures, scope changes and source-data refresh. Native board-column completion mapping and the sample eazyBI measure's Done status category at sprint close. Importing the required Team and estimate fields, issue drill-through and report copies with explicit definitions.

Instructor demo. Build or copy the sprint report, drill through its completed measure and inspect a seeded item counted differently by the board and the sample eazyBI measure (CRW-54 in ALR-2026.q4.3).

Practice: reconcile two conflicting totals. Align the team, sprint, filter and import time in the two report views. Trace the mismatch to a Jira item and its completion status or history. Save a report copy and record the measure definition and correction or next owner action.

Learner output. A reconciled report note with the two totals, reason, source key and the verified reporting definition.

4 · Evaluate release quality in Zephyr 40 min20 min practice

Objective. Use coverage and execution history to explain a release risk and the next test action.

Topics. Requirement-to-case coverage and cycle scope for a release or build. Failed, blocked and unexecuted tests, linked defects, regression selection and retest history. Quality review, Product Owner acceptance, UAT evidence and the approved completion point.

Instructor demo. Compare a cycle with many passes and a cycle that covers every required acceptance criterion. Trace a failure to its Jira Bug (CRW-62) and latest retest.

Practice: find the risk behind the pass rate. Identify one missing acceptance criterion and one unresolved test execution. Choose the tests to rerun after the supplied change and trace the associated Bug. Record a proceed, hold or clarify recommendation with the precise missing evidence.

Learner output. A coverage finding, a targeted retest choice and a release-quality recommendation.

Connect Jira to code and Claude · Modules 5–6 · 85 minutes

5 · Connect Jira to Git and verify traceability 45 min20 min practice

Objective. Explain the connection setup and trace a development change to the correct Jira work item.

Topics. The approved Git provider, integration permissions, repository selection and synchronization. Jira keys in branch names, commits and pull or merge requests, plus the Jira Development panel. Missing keys, wrong records, restricted repositories and related app context such as a Figma design link.

Instructor demo. With a platform or repository owner, show the approved repository connection. Create a branch and change with the sandbox Jira key and verify the Development panel. Keep a preconnected example ready (on this training site: the practice repository replica).

Practice: restore a missing code link. Inspect a seeded branch, commit and review and identify the missing or incorrect Jira key. Use the practice repository to correct the permitted reference and verify the Jira link. Explain which access or synchronization check to make if the expected activity still fails to appear.

Learner output. A Jira item with matching development activity or a documented diagnosis using the captured example.

6 · Connect Claude through Atlassian MCP 40 min20 min practice

Objective. Verify an approved connection and explain how identity, site and tool permissions govern its use.

Topics. Model Context Protocol, a Claude client and the Atlassian server's exposed Jira tools. Your organization's approved MCP setup and authentication flow, correct site selection and read, search and write scopes. When Rovo suits an Atlassian task and when Claude with MCP supports work in the approved external client.

Instructor demo. Follow the approved MCP setup guide, inspect the available tools and retrieve a known training item (CRW-25). Show how to diagnose an access-denied result without expanding the learner's permissions.

Practice: verify the client site and permissions. Complete or verify the approved connection using the distributed setup guide. Retrieve a known Jira item and check its key, URL and training site. Explain which tool permission supports the read and where to route a missing-access request.

Learner output. A verified item retrieval, source link and an explained permission boundary.

Verify AI results and decide · Modules 7–8 · 95 minutes

7 · Validate Claude queries and protect metric quality 55 min30 min practice

Objective. Check generated JQL and its result set before relying on a completeness or velocity claim.

Topics. Explicit project, team, sprint, work types, field IDs, result limits and the connected user's access. Data completeness definitions and why current-state JQL alone cannot reconstruct historical sprint velocity. Reviewing proposed writes, handling instructions embedded in work-item text and avoiding unintended bulk changes.

Instructor demo. Ask Claude for Stories and Bugs missing estimates in SWP-2026.q4.5. Show a plausible query that omits Bugs, then compare its results with the expected set (CRW-33, CRW-34) and the native sprint history.

Practice: catch the query that omits Bugs. Run the generated JQL in Jira and compare it with two expected missing-estimate items and an out-of-scope decoy (CRW-35). Correct the omitted work type and verify the count and keys, including any result truncation or access limitation. State the completeness denominator and verify a velocity claim in the native report. Identify the supplied item text that tries to redirect the task (CRW-34).

Learner output. The original and corrected query, expected and actual keys, metric definition and one identified risk.

8 · Make a release decision across tools 40 min35 min practice

Objective. Produce a concise recommendation that can be checked against the underlying Jira, planning, test and code records.

Topics. A delivery forecast, a reconciled measure, quality evidence and development traceability. A verified MCP result with the query and sources retained. The next action, accountable role and evidence needed to revisit a decision.

Instructor demo. Show the expected decision format and the distinction between an observed result and an unverified claim.

Practice: defend the release recommendation. Combine the chosen Plan scenario with the reconciled report and Zephyr coverage finding. Add the Jira-to-Git trace and the checked MCP query or retrieval. Give a three-minute proceed, hold or clarify recommendation in a peer exchange, with links, assumptions and the next action's role.

Learner output. A complete release decision worksheet and a short explanation that peers can verify from the source records.

On-demand preparation · 30 minutes

  • Review your organization's approved Atlassian MCP setup guide, confirm the Claude client and select the training Jira site.
  • Check Plans editing, eazyBI report-copy access, Zephyr evidence views and the practice Git repository. On this training site the last three are in-page practice replicas (Modules 3–5).
  • Open the supplied closed-sprint report (ALR-2026.q4.3 Sprint Report ↗) and note one discrepancy to investigate. Keep the setup guide for use after class.

Learners tick these off in the preparation checklist on the course home page.

Completion evidence

  • A Plan scenario comparison with downstream dependencies and receiving-team capacity checked.
  • A reconciled metric with the report scope, definition, import time and Jira keys recorded.
  • A validated Claude-generated query with expected and actual results and one corrected error.
  • A Jira development link and a release recommendation supported by planning and test evidence.

Learners fill an evidence card in each lab. My completion record collects them for print, PDF or Markdown submission. Use the submitted learner work for the agreed completion record.

Learner progression

Completion supports independent use of the approved planning, reporting and connector workflows within the learner's existing role. Route configuration changes and access requests to the responsible platform or app owner. Use the final evidence pack as the basis for team follow-up.

Golden Template placement

Use the approved template to diagnose field, status, parent-link and board-filter problems. Confirm its authority and any Crew-specific variations before the pilot. In this course the template is the reference for:

  • Status and completion: what Done means, and whether a board column may count Ready for Release as complete (Module 3).
  • Fields: Expected Business Impact, Outcome Evidence, Portfolio Size and Risk Treatment on the right work types (Module 1); Team and Story Points as the fields Plans and reports depend on (Modules 2, 3 and 7).
  • Parent links: Initiative → Epic → Story, so portfolio rollups and portfolioChildIssuesOf() work (Module 1).
  • Board filters: team filters by Team id, and the Kanban sub-filter that hides released work (Modules 1 and 3).

Cohort size

Up to 20 learners per cohort, with independent records for each learner or pair: one lab seat (CRP seats 01–20) and one personal plan scenario per learner or pair. Confirm cohort size, geography and scheduling before the pilot.
