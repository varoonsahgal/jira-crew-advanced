Over the last seven modules you have collected evidence that does not agree with itself: Plans shows a capacity risk, two reports give different totals, most tests pass, a code change was linked to the wrong work item and an assistant's query left out a Bug. M8.1 The decision format

A release recommendation has one job: let the accountable person make a decision and let anyone else check why. That means the answer comes first, every reason points to a record, and the next step has an owner. Long narratives fail that test. So do dashboards with no definitions.

Proceed

The evidence supports releasing as planned. Every material claim is observed in a source, and the remaining risks are known, small and owned.

Not the same as "nobody objected".

Hold

Observed evidence shows a risk that must be resolved before release: an overloaded sprint, a failing test on a required path, a blocker with no date.

Always name what would lift the hold.

Clarify

Evidence is missing or conflicts, so nobody can decide yet. Name the exact missing evidence, who supplies it and by when.

Not a polite way of saying "I don't know".

M8.2 Observed result or unverified claim?

Every sentence in a recommendation is one of four things. Tag each one, out loud and on the page. Most bad release decisions are not built on wrong data. They are built on an unverified claim that was repeated until it sounded observed.

Observed
You saw it in the source of record and can link to it: a JQL result, a report, a Plans view. Someone else can repeat it.
Observed in replica
You saw it in a practice replica or an exported view, not in the live app. Say so, and say how it will be confirmed.
Assumption
A condition you are choosing to rely on, stated so others can challenge it: "Swap Squad capacity stays at 15".
Unverified claim
Someone said it, or an assistant produced it, and you have not checked. It can be reported. It cannot carry the decision.
Assistants produce claims, not observationsA number Claude gives you is an unverified claim until you have run the query in Jira and compared the keys. That is why Module 7 matters here: only the corrected, re-run query earns the Observed tag.

Practice: tag the statement

project = CRW AND fixVersion = "Crew App 4.2" AND statusCategory != Done returns 10 work items when run today (link attached).

A query you ran, with a link anyone can open. That is an observation, valid as of the time you ran it.

"Claude says Swap Squad delivered 21 points in SWP-2026.q4.2, so the q4.5 load is fine."

An assistant's number that you haven't checked, and in fact it's wrong: the Velocity report says 18 for q4.2.

Swap Squad's capacity for SWP-2026.q4.5 is 15 points.

This is the planning input you chose in your scenario. State it so someone can challenge it.

"Vendor Management says the rules vendor sandbox credentials (CRW-46) will arrive before 25 November."

Someone said it. CRW-46 is still in Development and flagged. Report the claim; don't build the forecast on it.

The ALR-2026.q4.3 Sprint Report lists CRW-54 and CRW-55 as completed with status Ready for Release.

You can open the report and see it. The interpretation, that these weren't Done at close, comes from the board mapping you checked in Module 3.

In the Zephyr practice cycle, the swap-approved email case's latest execution failed and is linked to Bug CRW-62.

The test execution came from the practice replica. CRW-62 itself is live in Jira and can be linked. Repeat the check in Zephyr once it is installed.

"The eazyBI number must be right because it's the official reporting tool."

A tool's authority is not a definition. Without the measure definition and import time, this is an opinion about a number.

Instructor demo · 5 min
  1. Show the worksheet format with a deliberately different decision, so you don't hand the room the answer: "Should Swap Squad pull CRW-42 into SWP-2026.q4.4?"
  2. Read two sentences aloud and tag them live. "CRW-25 is blocked by CRW-46" (Observed: open the link). "The credentials will come Monday" (Unverified claim: who said it, where is it recorded?).
  3. Show what turns a claim into an observation: run project = CRW AND (Flagged is not EMPTY OR issueLinkType = "is blocked by") AND statusCategory != Done and point at the 5 keys.
  4. Show a next-action line with a role, a date and a revisit trigger, and one without. Ask which one a sponsor can act on.
  5. Start the three-minute timer and model the opening: decision first, three reasons with sources, the ask, the owner.

Checkpoint

1. Which statement can be tagged Observed in a release recommendation?

An observation is something you saw in the source of record and someone else can repeat. An assistant's count, a team's confidence and an inference from an average are claims or assumptions.

2. When is "Clarify" the right decision?

Clarify is a decision with a specific ask. Without the missing evidence, owner and date, it's just a delay.

3. Option A (move CRW-31 to SWP-2026.q4.6) makes Swap Squad's sprint fit. What must your forecast line also say?

A forecast checks both teams, dependency order and the release marker. In the course you never save scenario changes; you review and discard them.

4. Alerts Squad's q4.3 shows 19 completed on the board and 11 in the sample eazyBI measure. How do you quote it?

Both numbers are correct under their own definitions. A reconciled measure states the definition, the alternative and the source keys.

5. Which next-action line is complete?

A next action needs an accountable role, a date and the evidence that would change the decision. The last option is also an unreviewed bulk write.
