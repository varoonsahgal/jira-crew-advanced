Course at a glance

The hardest leg of the series

Every number in a release review has a definition, a scope and a source record behind it. This course trains you to find all three before you sign your name under a recommendation.

6 hoursof instruction and practice: one full day or two half-days, with breaks added
195 minof hands-on practice across 8 labs
Blendedlive virtual teaching, demos and sandbox exercises, plus 30 minutes of on-demand preparation
≤ 20learners per cohort, with a lab seat per learner or pair

Who it's for

Delivery leads, product owners, reporting authors, QA leads, engineers and approved Claude users. You already run sprints and read reports. Here you decide what those reports are allowed to prove.

What you need first

Intermediate skills or equivalent evidence of verified JQL, sprint reporting, Plan capacity and Zephyr execution. You use the approved Claude client. Engineers completing the Git exercise also need basic branch, commit and review skills.

Classroom scenario

Four days before the release review

It's Sunday 22 November 2026. Crew Scheduling is building Crew Self-Service 2027 (CRW-1 ↗), an Initiative that promises 40% fewer scheduler-handled swap requests and swap turnaround from 2 days to under 2 hours by Q2 2027. Two teams share the work: Swap Squad builds the swap flow and Alerts Squad sends the notifications crew rely on. Crew App 4.2 is due on 18 December. Leadership wants a recommendation: proceed, hold or clarify.

The portfolioEpic CRW-5 ↗ Swap Request Status Tracking shipped in Crew App 4.1 on 30 October. Every child is Done. It sits in Monitored with an empty Outcome Evidence field. Can anyone close it yet?
The forecastPlans shows a capacity risk. Swap Squad's capacity for SWP-2026.q4.5 drops from 20 to 15 points, but the sprint holds 18. Moving the obvious story, CRW-31 ↗, turns the capacity bar green and the Crew App 4.2 marker red, and leaves Alerts Squad's CRW-73 ↗ scheduled 25 days before the rule it depends on.
The reportsTwo reports disagree. Alerts Squad's board says ALR-2026.q4.3 completed 19 points. The sample eazyBI measure says 11. The 8-point gap is two work items, and only one of them is actually Done today.
The testsMost tests pass. One cycle shows 93%, yet an acceptance criterion about crew personal data has no executed test, and the swap-approved email case keeps failing against Bug CRW-62 ↗.
The codeA code change has no Jira link, and another commit points at the wrong work item. Neither shows up where the release review looks.
The assistantClaude, connected through Atlassian MCP, writes a confident query for missing estimates. It quietly leaves out Bugs. One of those Bugs carries a note addressed to AI assistants, asking them to tidy the sprint's numbers.

You investigate each claim, record what you observed and what you only assumed, and defend a recommendation that a peer can check against the source records in three minutes.

Golden Template placementUse the approved Golden Template as the reference when you diagnose problems: which field a value belongs in, which status means done, how parent links build the hierarchy and how board filters decide what a board and its reports can see. In this course it explains why CRW-5 needed its board sub-filter cleared (Module 1), why Alerts Squad's board counts Ready for Release as complete (Module 3) and why Team = "Swap Squad" silently matches nothing (Modules 3 and 7). Confirm the template's authority and any Crew-specific variations with the template owner before you treat a difference as a defect.

Cabin rules

How we fly together

Twenty investigators, one reference spaceCRW is read-only. Every Jira write you make happens in your CRP seat: label lab-{SEAT}, summary prefix L{SEAT} · , never another seat's records. In Plans you work only in your own scenario Lab {SEAT}, and you discard your changes. Nobody presses Save.
Run in Jira buttonsEvery JQL block has a Run in Jira ↗ button that opens the query on the training site. Run it, compare the count and keys with the page, and write down any difference. A difference is a finding, not a failure.
Observed or claimed?Throughout the course you tag each statement as something you observed (with a link or query), an assumption you state openly, or an unverified claim. AI answers, dashboards and confident colleagues all go in the last pile until you check them.
